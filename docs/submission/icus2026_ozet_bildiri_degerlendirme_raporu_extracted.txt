CONFIDENTIAL COMMENTS TO THE EDITOR   (copy the prose below)
########################################################################
I recommend conditional acceptance of this abstract, with revisions to
the claims and methodological specificity. The submission proposes an
open-source, QGIS-based typomorphological workflow that links street-scale
urban fabric metrics to climate resilience indicators across the Izmir
Gulf -- a topic that aligns well with the Climate Change Resilient Cities
theme. The dual-unit spatial design (pedestrian catchments overlaid with
morphological cells) and the four-stage analytical pipeline are
thoughtfully conceived and reflect genuine methodological literacy in
spatial analysis, urban morphology, and open-source GIS. The abstract
suffers from a structural tension: it is written as if reporting completed
work, yet no results, proof-of-concept outputs, or preliminary validation
are provided for an abstract-only submission. Several strong claims (an
explainable morphometric synthesis, decision-support-ready outputs, a
repeatable and auditable model) outpace what the abstract can demonstrate.
The methodological section lists numerous indicators but leaves critical
operational choices unspecified -- the clustering method, the
dimensionality reduction technique, the weighting scheme for
multi-criteria prioritisation, and the mechanism by which the two spatial
layers are analytically integrated. The abstract is strong enough for
presentation at ICUS 2026, but the authors should either add preliminary
results or recalibrate the claims to the proposal stage. The scientific
risk to the session is low; the methodological design is competent and the
topic is timely. If the authors deliver even a subset of the promised
workflow with empirical outputs, the presentation will be a solid
contribution to the conference.
COMMENTS TO THE AUTHORS
This abstract proposes an ambitious and well-structured open-source
workflow that couples urban fabric morphometrics with climate resilience
assessment at street scale across the Izmir Gulf. The dual spatial-unit
design -- pedestrian catchments derived from road-centre-line networks
alongside morphological cells from building footprints -- is the
manuscript's strongest conceptual move, because it forces the analysis to
read movement potential and built-form intensity together rather than
reducing one to the other. The four-stage pipeline (data harmonisation,
street-network analytics, building/cell morphometrics, climate-resilience
proxies) is logically sequenced, and the commitment to open-source QGIS
and reproducible project structure is appropriate for the venue and the
stated goal of producing a transferable decision-support model.
The abstract has one structural problem that shapes most of the comments
below: it is written as a completed study but contains no results,
proof-of-concept outputs, or validation. Conference abstracts that promise
an atlas, a set of fabric classes, and intervention priorities without
showing even a single preliminary map or classification place the reviewer
in the position of evaluating a proposal rather than a submission. The
method is detailed enough to be credible -- the list of indicators is
extensive, the analytical stages are sensible -- but the operational
choices that would let a reader assess feasibility are largely absent. The
comments below follow the manuscript order; the most consequential are
the results gap (Major 1), the dual-layer integration mechanism (Major 2),
and the overstatement of readiness (Major 3).
MAJOR COMMENTS
Abstract framing and scope:
1. The abstract promises outputs that the submission does not contain.
The English and Turkish abstracts both state that the study will produce
an open-source QGIS atlas, a set of street-scale urban fabric classes, and
a prioritisation logic for climate adaptation interventions -- yet no
results, preliminary maps, proof-of-concept outputs, or illustrative
fabric classes appear anywhere in the submission. For an abstract-only
submission that states "full paper will not be submitted to the congress,"
the gap between promised outputs and presented evidence is structural.
Either add at least one proof-of-concept map, a pilot classification from
a single neighbourhood, or an illustrative fabric-resilience profile from
one sample cluster; or reframe the abstract in future tense throughout and
state explicitly that the workflow design is the contribution and the
empirical outputs are anticipated. The current framing risks a
presentation that cannot meet the expectations the abstract sets.
2. The dual-unit spatial structure is the most innovative element of the
design, but the relationship between the two layers is not analytically
resolved. The abstract states that 400- and 800-metre pedestrian
catchments form the first layer and morphological cells and sub-block
fabric partitions form the second, and that "both movement potential and
built-form intensity can be read together." How they are read together is
not stated. Are morphometric indicators computed within each catchment and
then attributed to catchment units? Are the two layers modelled
hierarchically (cells nested in catchments)? Are the cells used only for
built-form metrics while catchments carry network metrics, producing two
parallel but unintegrated profiles? Specify the analytical integration
mechanism -- a hierarchical model, a cross-attribution rule, a zonal
statistics operation -- in one or two concrete sentences.
Methodological specificity:
3. The indicator set is listed as a catalogue of metric names but lacks
the operational detail needed to assess feasibility or comparability.
Connectivity, node density, meshedness, orientation entropy, circuity,
angular integration, and angular choice are all named for the street
network; ground coverage, floor-space intensity proxies, openness ratio,
compactness, frontage continuity, block permeability, cell heterogeneity,
and open-space fragmentation are all named for the building/cell layer.
None carries a formula, a computational reference, a unit, or a stated
source package (e.g., QGIS processing toolbox, Python library, GRASS
module). For an abstract that foregrounds reproducibility, name the
specific tool or library for at least the core indicators so a reader can
assess whether the workflow is genuinely reproducible or only conceptually
described.
4. The dimensionality reduction, clustering, and multi-criteria
prioritisation steps that produce the fabric-resilience profiles are
mentioned only in passing. The English abstract states: "dimensionality
reduction, unsupervised clustering and multi-criteria prioritisation are
then used to classify samples and identify dominant adaptation drivers."
No algorithm is named (PCA? t-SNE? UMAP? for reduction; k-means?
hierarchical? DBSCAN? for clustering; AHP? TOPSIS? weighted sum? for
prioritisation). These choices are consequential: different methods produce
different classifications, and an "explainable morphometric synthesis"
depends on the transparency of the combinatorial step. State the specific
algorithms and the rationale for each.
5. Scale sensitivity is acknowledged but not addressed. The abstract
states that indicators are "standardised with attention to scale effects,"
and the catchments are defined at two sizes (400 m and 800 m), but there
is no indication of how scale effects are diagnosed or corrected. For
example, block permeability computed on a 400 m versus 800 m catchment
will differ systematically; a fabric type defined at one catchment radius
may not survive at the other. State how the two catchment sizes are used
in the classification -- are they alternative analytical grains run in
parallel, or is one nested in the other? Report whether and how the
fabric-resilience profiles are stable across the two scales.
6. The social vulnerability component is listed among the climate and
resilience proxies but its operationalisation is the least specified part
of the abstract. The English text names "socio-spatial vulnerability
indicators"; the Turkish text adds "sosyal kirilganlik gostergeleri." No
source, variable, index, or aggregation logic is stated. Social
vulnerability indices range from simple (e.g., census-based age and income
proportions) to composite (e.g., SoVI, SVI, or bespoke PCA-based indices),
and the choice materially affects which populations and places are flagged
as vulnerable. Specify the vulnerability data source, the indicators,
and the aggregation method.
7. The climate hazards the resilience assessment targets are not
enumerated. The abstract invokes heat exposure (golgelenme/gunelenme,
shadow/solar exposure), emergency access (toplanma alani, assembly areas),
and green-blue infrastructure proximity, which collectively point toward
urban heat and possibly flood pathways -- but the specific hazards are
never named. A resilience framework that does not state which climate
stressors it addresses cannot be assessed for whether the chosen
indicators match the hazard. State the target hazards explicitly (e.g.,
extreme heat, pluvial flooding, coastal inundation) and show that the
indicator set is hazard-appropriate.
Study design and sampling:
8. The stratified sampling design is well-motivated conceptually (historic
centres, grids, apartment blocks, waterfront transformation zones,
hillside settlements, industrial-logistics fragments, peripheral
expansion) but the sampling logic is not described. How many street
segments or catchments per fabric type? What is the total N? Are the
strata defined a priori from planning documents or land-use maps, or are
they emergent from the morphological clustering itself? If the latter, the
design is circular: samples are assigned to types that the analysis is
supposed to discover. Clarify the stratification source and the sample
size per stratum.
9. The abstract states that morphological cells serve "as a proxy for
fine-grained plot/building relationships where cadastral data are
unavailable or inconsistent." This is a defensible workaround, but the
validity of the proxy is not discussed. Morphological cells derived from
building footprints alone cannot capture plot boundaries, ownership
parcels, or development rights -- all of which affect urban form and
climate adaptation capacity. State the limitations of the
building-footprint-only proxy and, if possible, provide a small validation
against available cadastral data for at least one sub-area.
Conceptual clarity:
10. The term "urban fabric" anchors the abstract but is used in at least
three distinct senses: as a descriptive category (historic centre fabric,
grid fabric), as a measurable morphological profile (the morphometric
indicators), and as an explanatory mechanism (fabric explains resilience
outcomes). The slippage between these senses makes the argument hard to
track. Distinguish fabric-as-taxon (the type label) from
fabric-as-measurement (the indicator vector) from fabric-as-mechanism
(the causal claim) -- or at minimum use the terminology consistently so
the reader knows which sense is active at each point.
11. The contribution claim -- "coupling urban morphology, street-network
analytics and climate-resilience planning" -- is reasonable but needs to
be positioned against existing work that already couples some of these
elements. The urban morphology community (e.g., the ISUF tradition,
Space Syntax applications to resilience, Morpho and Urban Morpho metrics)
and the spatial analytics community (e.g., Cityseer, momepy, the Urban
Morphometrics toolkit) have produced open-source morphometric workflows
that the abstract does not acknowledge. Cite the closest prior work and
state what this workflow adds that the existing toolchains do not.
MINOR COMMENTS
Abstract and title:
a) The Turkish title uses "Iklim Direncliligi" and the English uses
"Climate Resilience" -- both are appropriate. However, the English title
runs to 19 words and 145 characters with spaces, which is long for a
conference abstract title. Consider shortening, e.g., dropping "An
Open-Source QGIS-Based" if QGIS is already stated in the abstract body.
b) The English abstract word count is reported as 524 words and the
Turkish as 517. Verify that the conference limit is 500-750 as stated in
the submission compliance table; some ICUS venues use a 500-word ceiling.
c) The keywords in both languages include "kentsel doku / urban fabric"
and "acik kaynak QGIS / open-source QGIS." The title already contains both
terms. Conference abstract keywords should extend beyond the title to
capture dimensions the title misses -- consider adding "spatial analysis,"
"GIS," "morphometrics," "pedestrian accessibility," or "vulnerability" to
improve discoverability without duplication.
d) The Turkish abstract uses "kentsel doku" throughout (consistent with
the stated terminology decision). The submission compliance table notes
that "urban tissue" is reserved as a secondary morphology term. This is a
careful terminological choice worth keeping. Verify that no instance of
"doku" has drifted in meaning -- in one Turkish sentence "kentsel dokunun
olculebilir morfolojik yapisi" could be read as either fabric or texture.
Methodological language:
e) "Angular integration" and "angular choice" are Space Syntax terms that
assume a segment-map representation of the street network. State whether
the analysis uses a segment map (standard in depthmapX and the QGIS Space
Syntax Toolkit) or a centre-line representation, because the two produce
different values. If a centre-line network is used but segment-based
metrics are computed, the conversion step should be noted.
f) "Floor-space intensity proxies" is vague. If this means
floor-area-ratio or building-volume-to-site-area, name the proxy. If the
data do not support floor counts and the proxy is building footprint area
relative to cell area, state that explicitly.
g) "Impervious surface exposure" is listed as a climate proxy. Clarify
whether this is measured from satellite-derived imperviousness data
(e.g., Copernicus Imperviousness Density, ESA WorldCover) or approximated
from building footprint density, because the two carry different
uncertainties.
Presentation and language:
h) The English abstract is well-written and largely free of grammatical
errors. One sentence in the opening paragraph is unusually long at 51
words: "Focusing on the Izmir Gulf, the study integrates street-network
configuration, building pattern, open-space continuity, pedestrian
accessibility, shadow and solar exposure proxies, green-blue
infrastructure proximity, emergency/service access and social
vulnerability within a single typomorphological framework." Consider
breaking this into two sentences for readability.
i) The Turkish abstract consistently uses the passive voice ("kurgulanmistir",
"derlenmekte", "hesaplanmaktadir", "olculmektedir", "eklenmektedir") which
is conventional for Turkish academic prose and appropriate for the venue.
No change needed.
j) The submission compliance table reports "Tables, figures, references in
abstract body: None" -- this is appropriate. However, if the conference
format permits a single schematic figure, adding a workflow diagram would
substantially improve the abstract's clarity given the four-stage
pipeline. The current text-only description of the pipeline requires the
reader to hold four stages of indicator types in working memory.
References and data:
k) No data sources are cited with DOIs or persistent identifiers. The
abstract mentions "open geospatial layers" and "open data sources" but
does not name a single one -- OpenStreetMap, Copernicus Urban Atlas,
Turkish Statistical Institute, Esri World Imagery, any specific building
footprint dataset. For a submission whose core claim is open-source
reproducibility, name at minimum the building footprint source and the
street network source.

climate resilience are a substantive extension of existing urban
morphometrics work into the resilience domain, applied to a
well-motivated Turkish coastal metropolitan context. The contribution
is a novel framework integration rather than a novel method, and the
absence of results or prior-art positioning tempers the score.
Methodological Validity: 55 / 100 -- The four-stage pipeline is logically
coherent, the indicator families are appropriate for the research
question, and the commitment to open-source reproducibility is a
strength. However, no indicators are operationally defined; the
integration mechanism between the two spatial layers is unspecified;
the clustering, dimensionality reduction, and prioritisation algorithms
are not named; scale sensitivity is mentioned but not resolved; and no
validation strategy is proposed. The methodological description is a
well-organised catalogue but not yet a testable protocol.
Presentation: 72 / 100 -- The bilingual abstract is clearly written,
the structure follows a logical sequence (gap, design, method,
contribution), and the terminology is carefully managed with an
explicit terminology decision. The abstract reads cleanly and the
technical ambition is communicated effectively. Shortcomings include
an excessively long English title, a single 51-word sentence, and the
absence of even a schematic workflow diagram that would substantially
aid comprehension.
